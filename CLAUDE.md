# BoardTestG

The 2D board of BoardTestRK (`../BoardTestRK`), reimplemented in Godot 4 (GDScript). If this works well, more of
BoardTestRK moves here. For now: the 2D board and the 3D cube (switch in Settings > Board > Board Type), Subtract mode (tap a group of 3 or more).

## Rules

The game rules are the same as BoardTestRK's. Read `../BoardTestRK/BoardTestRKRules.md` first. This project covers
its 2D-board parts: groups, removal, gravity, refill, Blockers, no-moves recovery, and the Fly Away effect
(here real rigid-body physics: pieces fall to the floor, bounce off the board and each other, then fade). Not yet
ported: Add mode (drag swaps), the idle hint, the rounded cube / diamond / ellipsoid / extruded-letter / joined shapes, sounds, the sphere and solid boards. The 3D cube (`CubeModel`/`CubeView`) is done; the four joined shapes work on it too (`CubeJoins`). Block shapes done: Cube, Sphere, Cone, Cylinder, Glow, Rounded Cube, Joined Cubes / Joined Spheres (from BoardTestRK's Joined Squares / Circles) Joined Cylinders (new) and Joined Glow (new: streaks of light between same-colour neighbours) (`BuildShapes` in BoardView.gd; a shape is a mesh, a black-edge twin and a collider).

## Layout

Scripts are grouped by job, each file small and about one thing:

- `Scripts/Model/BoardModel.gd` - the rules, no nodes. Cells are (Col, Row), Row 0 at the top. Falling is worked out in
  a "virtual" frame where gravity always points to higher rows (`ToReal` maps back); `Queue` holds new pieces off the
  board. `Drop()` returns each moved piece with its path of real cells.
- `Scripts/Model/CubeModel.gd` - the rules of the 3D cube, no nodes (BoardTestRK's cube shape): a hollow Width x Height x Depth box (3 to 15 each) whose surface cells hold the pieces, one piece per surface cell (`Cells`, keyed by the (X, Y, Z) cell), so edge and corner pieces are shared by the faces they touch. Gravity is the real, world one (down on screen, however the cube is turned; `Drop(Gravity)` takes it in the cube's own space, `CubeView.Gravity()`). A gap is filled by the piece above it falling in (`BuildFeeders`: a neighbour whose way to the gap is more than `SlopeMin` aligned with gravity), so pieces run down a face, round an edge and onto the next, and rest on a level face. New pieces enter only at the *top*, wherever that currently is: a cell that faces the sky (`IsExposed`, `SkyMin`) and has nothing above it to fall from gets a new piece that falls in from above (`SpawnAtSources`; `Depth` staggers a column of them) - so as the cube turns, the new top surface is the one that is fed. A gap neither can reach (a level underside) is filled by a piece sliding in along the surface from a neighbour that is not lower, never into or out of a sky-facing cell (`SlideIntoGaps`), and the gap that leaves is filled from the top; only a gap with no neighbour to slide from grows a piece in place. Each face is still a grid of (Col, Row) (`FaceCell`) for counting and tests. Groups flood across edges (a shared piece counts once). No Blockers, no diagonal moves (as in RK). `Drop()` returns each moved piece with its path of cells.
- `Scripts/Style/Backgrounds.gd` + `Shaders/*.gdshader` - Settings > Scene > Background: Gray (plain color + checker floor, the default), Sky (clouds), Night (slowly twinkling stars), Star Flight (stars drifting out from the middle in perspective). The last three are screen-space sky shaders (so they fill the window, not just the sliver above the horizon);  the checker floor stays for all of them; `SceneSetup.SetBackground` applies one, and a drawn sky is also what glossy pieces reflect. `--background=N` picks one in a capture.
- `Scripts/Style/` - how things look and what they collide as. `BlockStyles.gd` (the shapes: mesh, black-edge twin,
  collider; the color and Glow materials; `MakeVisual`/`ApplyShape`), `BlockerLight.gd` (the pulsing indigo Blocker: a depth-tested ball of light, shaded by facing so it is round from any side, with camera-facing zaps), `LightningEffect.gd` (jagged re-jittering bolts joining a cleared group, plus a flash; plays for `LightningLead` before the pieces fly out, toggle "Lightning"; on the 3D cube the bolts are depth-tested and start just outside each piece's face, so the cube's blocks hide them - `CubeView.BoltLift`),
  `PhysicsLayers.gd` (collision layers).
- `Scripts/View/IdleHint.gd` - the idle hint (from BoardTestRK): after 20 s with no input, not in Attract Mode and not while busy, the largest playable group (ties: lowest on the board) gets a 5x5 unlit checkerboard in black and the group's own color (all hint shaders take a `Tint`); a hint stays until a piece on the board is tapped (`BoardView.HandleTapRay` calls `Hint.Dismiss`); other input only restarts the idle clock (`Main._input` calls `Hint.RecordActivity`). Not shown for the Glow shape. `Scripts/View/JoinSystem.gd` - the joined shapes: same-colour neighbours that touch and are both at rest are joined (cubes grow to meet each other; spheres/cylinders get bridges), moving or removed pieces lose their joins, they come back on landing; `BoardView` calls `Joins.Update(MovingIds)` on tap, drop, landing and shuffle. `Scripts/View/BoardView.gd` - draws the model, taps and turns, animates drops (same speed for every piece; the longest
  drop takes 0.35 s), refills and shuffles. `Scripts/View/CubeView.gd` - the 3D cube's view, with the same outside as `BoardView` (signals, `NewGame`, `HandleTapRay`, `AttractStep`, `ShuffleNow`, `Recenter`, `FrameDistance`/`FrameMiddle`) so `Main` can drive either: taps are ray-cast against the box and give the surface cell hit; dragging turns the cube in screen space (`CubeView.RotateBy`: sideways about the world's vertical, up/down about the screen's horizontal), so the face you see always follows the mouse or finger, whatever way it is turned; `Recenter` (and a new game) turns the face with the most pieces to the camera (`FaceTurns` = the Yaw/Tilt for each face). No idle hint on the cube. `Scripts/View/CubeJoins.gd` - the joined shapes on the cube: the same rules as `JoinSystem` but with six neighbour bits (the same bits `PieceMeshes.AddBox` uses to skip faces), so a group joins round an edge too. `Scripts/View/FlyerSystem.gd` - removed pieces as `RigidBody3D`s (launch,
  gravity, fade, the stand-ins that block a piece's own gap). `Scripts/View/SmokeBurst.gd` - the Puff of Smoke removal effect (see Removal effects).
- `Scripts/App/` - `Main.gd` (wires it together; mouse input), `ControlBar.gd` (score/FPS, the gear button at the top right and the grouped, touch-sized Settings window it opens; reports by signals), `GearButton.gd` (the drawn gear), `CameraButton.gd` (Mac only, under the gear: `Main.TakeScreenshot` hides the controls and saves a PNG to ~/Pictures),
  `SceneSetup.gd` (sky, light, camera, floor), `GameSettings.gd` (what is remembered), `CaptureRunner.gd` (the
  `--capture` mode and the other startup options below; `--settings` opens the Settings window in a capture).
- `Tests/TestBoardModel.gd`, `Tests/TestCubeModel.gd`, `Tests/TestCubeView.gd` (tap rays and face turns; needs a frame, so it runs in `_process`) - headless checks.

## Play modes and levels

`Scripts/Game/`: `GameMode` (Zen default, Timed, Turn - how the user plays, chosen in Settings > Game > Play Mode, saved as `PlayMode`; changing it starts a new game), `LevelDefinition` (Difficulty 1-10, ShapeType 2D/3D, Dimensions, `ColorCount` (0 = the Settings value), PresetPieces/PresetBlockers/PresetSpecials, TurnCount, TimeCount, and the goal `ColorGoals`: a list of Vector2i(color, pieces to remove)), `GameSession` (a `Node` owned by `Main`: turns, elapsed time, `Remaining` pieces per goal color, Playing/Won/Lost). **The goal is color removal**: remove that many pieces of each goal color; colors not listed are not part of the goal. A turn is a tap that removes a group; both boards emit `GroupRemoved(Kind, Size)` and `Main` connects it to `GameSession.RecordRemoval`. `Default()` (no level picked) is 45 red pieces, 17 turns or 45 s. The top left shows one `ColorGoalBadge` per goal color still to go (a circle in the color with the pieces left inside; a checkmark replaces the number when that color is done - `ControlBar.SetGoals`, `ColorGoalBadge`) and the clock (turns left / time left; none in Zen); there is no score readout. The 2D idle hint is for the largest group of a color still wanted (`IdleHint.WantedKinds`, `BoardModel.LargestGroup(Wanted)`; any group if none of those is playable). The clock starts at the first removal (so a Timed level waits for the first group) and waits while Settings is open. The level number is centered at the top (`ControlBar.SetLevel`). Turning Attract Mode on starts a new game with the Settings window's board values (size, colors, Blockers) instead of a level (`Main.OnAttractToggled`); Attract Mode has no goal, turn limit or time limit (`GameSession.Unlimited`): it plays while any group can be removed, and when none is left (`NoPlayableGroups`, emitted by the views' `FinishDrop` instead of an automatic shuffle) shows "Game over", and 2 s later every piece explodes (as on a won level) and, once the shards have settled, a new game fills the board with new pieces - `Main.OnNoPlayableGroups`. Timed/Turn: finishing every goal color wins (`Main.WinSequence`: once the last drop has settled every piece explodes (including removed pieces still flying about or fading, which shatter where they are) and any Blockers fly off out of sight on random curving paths (`BlockerLight.FlyAway`, 0.8 s, the next level waits for them) - `ExplodeAll` on the views, `FlyerSystem.ExplodeAll` shares a shard budget - and when the shards are gone the next level starts; after the last level, Level 1; `Main.SequenceId` drops a sequence whose game was replaced), running out loses, and then taps (and Attract Mode) stop; a lost level shows a "Replay? Yes No" prompt near the bottom (`ControlBar.ReplayPrompt`; until it is answered nothing else works: `InputLocked` on the boards stops taps, shuffles, Attract moves and the hint, and the Settings gear and camera are disabled; Yes plays the level again, No goes back to Attract Mode; not shown in Attract Mode, which restarts by itself); Zen never ends and plays on after the goal.

`Levels.gd` reads the list at run time from JSON (see **Level files** below and `Levels/README.md`; seventeen levels ship: Classic 1-10, Irregular 1-5, Countdown 1-2) (every launch starts in Attract Mode on the Settings board with a "Play" button (`ControlBar.PlayButton`, shown whenever Attract Mode is on; the goal and clock are hidden then), and Play starts `Main.ResumeLevel`: Level 1 at first, afterwards the level that was being played when Attract Mode came on - a failed level answered "No", or one interrupted by turning Attract Mode on in Settings. `--level=N` skips the title. The game is always on the 2D board at launch) (`Table` = width x height, colors dealt 4-6, goals; difficulty = level number; board 3-15 x 3-13; some colors left out of each goal; at most one Blocker per 40 cells, cells chosen once by a seeded `BoardModel` and stored in `PresetBlockers`; turns = pieces to remove / 1.6 (`PiecesPerTurn`, set from what the difficulty test's bot achieved) plus a fudge of 25% (biggest board) to 50% (smallest) that grows as board pieces get fewer, `TurnsFor`; time = 1 s per piece). Settings > Game > Level plays one (switches to the 2D board; `Main.LevelIndex`, -1 = the Settings board with `LevelDefinition.Default`); `BoardModel`/`BoardView.NewGame` take the preset Blocker cells. Other presets (pieces, specials) are defined but not applied. Test: `Tests/TestGameSession.gd`.

**Malicious** (Settings > Malicious; four switches, each saved as `Malicious*` in `GameSettings`, applied by `Main.ApplyMalicious`/`MaliciousStep`): (1) the sun spins 360 degrees about the vertical in 15 s, in the direction that decreases `LightAngle` (the opposite of the board's Auto Rotate) (`Main.LightPeriod`, `SceneSetup.Sun`, yaw added to `SunDirection`); (2) Blockers move after every group (`BoardView.BlockersMove`: `StartDrop` calls `RelocateBlockers` -> `BoardModel.RelocateBlockers`, which re-places the same number at random with the open cells connected, deletes pieces under new Blockers, and the drop that follows backfills the cells they left; 2D only); (3) a veil: every 20 s of play one large zero-thickness sheet (`Veil`, a multiply-blend quad) comes down over the whole set of pieces like a colored filter, its color the complement of one piece color - a color the goal still needs, else any - so that color goes nearly black and the rest are tinted; it fades in, and lifts 10 s later. On the 2D board it is a child of the board in front of its face, so it turns and foreshortens with the board; on the cube it is a world-space sheet facing the camera, refitted every frame to the cube's projected outline (`Veil.Place`). `--veil` in `CaptureRunner` shows it; the tiles are children of the pieces (a plate in front on the 2D board, a wrapping box on the cube), and new pieces of that color get one while it is down (`VeilOn`/`VeilOff` on both views, timing in `Main.MaliciousStep`, clock paused outside play and in Settings); (4) the board is turned 45 degrees (`LockedYaw`) and cannot be turned: `TurnLocked` makes `RotateBy`, Auto Rotate and the cube's Attract spin do nothing, and Recenter (button or double tap) returns to the locked pose.

**Difficulty test** (Settings > Level Difficulty > Start; `DifficultyRun`, `DifficultyOverlay`): a bot plays every level 30 times on `BoardModel` alone (one trial per frame, so the overlay shows the level, trial, running mean and a line per finished level; Cancel keeps finished levels). Each move is the largest group of a color the goal still needs (as the hint), else the largest group of any color; a trial counts its turns (cap 300). `Levels.Record` stores the mean turns and win rate in the level (`LevelDefinition.MeanTurns`, `WinRate`) and in `user://LevelDifficulty.cfg` (the `Table` in the source is a constant, so results live beside it and are applied by `Levels.LoadStats`), and sets `Difficulty` from mean turns / allowed turns (`Levels.DifficultyFor`: ratio 0.3 or less = 1, 1.0 or more = 10).

## Irregular boards (gaps)

A 2D board can have **gaps**: cells that are not part of it (no piece, no Blocker, nothing drawn), as in other Match-3 games. A layout is a picture, one string per row (`#` cell, `.` gap), in `Scripts/Game/BoardLayouts.gd` (Diamond, Cross, Hourglass, Heart, Ring, Octagon; the open cells of a layout must stay connected - the test checks it). It is defined the way a rectangle is: `BoardLayouts.Size` is its Width x Height and `BoardLayouts.Gaps` the gap cells, which `LevelDefinition.PresetGaps` and `BoardModel`'s last constructor argument (`Gaps[Col][Row]`, `IsGap`, `IsOpen`) take; `BoardView.NewGame(..., PresetBlockers, Gaps)`. Settings > Board > Layout picks one for the Settings board (saved as `BoardLayout`, an index into `BoardLayouts.Names()`, 0 = plain Width x Height; width and height are hidden while one is picked; `Main.OnLayoutSelected`); `Levels.Table` rows take either a `Vector2i` size or a layout name, and levels 11-15 are irregular (Diamond, Cross, Hourglass, Heart, Ring; `Levels.InitialDifficulty` gives them 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 until the difficulty test measures them). `--layout=Name` picks one in a capture/launch.

Falling with gaps (`BoardModel`, in the virtual frame): a gap with only gaps above it in its column (as gravity points) is open air (`VAir`) - new pieces fall through it into the first real cell, so a diamond's short columns are fed from above. A gap with real cells above it (a ring's hole) is solid (`VSolid`) like a Blocker: it seals the cells under it (`ColumnSealed`), which are fed by pieces sliding in diagonally. Pieces never slide diagonally into or out of a gap cell. A cell nothing can reach (under a hole two cells or more wide, or beside a gap under a Blocker) would stay empty for good, so `Drop` ends with `FillDeadCells`: a new piece in place (a two-entry path on the same cell, so the view pops it in). A piece left in open air is discarded in `DiscardQueued`. The Malicious veil still covers the whole rectangle. Tests: `TestBoardModel.TestGaps` (every layout x four gravities x 0/2 Blockers), `TestGameSession` (the five irregular levels).

## Turning the board

`BoardView` is the board's pivot (its middle): `Yaw` about the world's vertical, then `Tilt` about the board's own
horizontal axis, so no part of it can dip below the floor. Gravity stays world-down. Cells are in the board's own
space (`CellPosition`), taps are ray-cast into that space, and removed pieces live in `FlyerSystem` (world space, a sibling
of the board) so they do not turn with it. A removed piece always falls straight down in the world - the way gravity pulls,
the shortest way to the floor, through whatever is beneath it - whatever way the board is turned. Gravity on the board is down *as the user sees it*, however the board is turned (unlike BoardTestRK, whose 2D board always falls along its own down): before each drop `GravityFromUser` takes the camera's screen-down into the board's space and rounds it to one of four grid directions (`BoardModel.GravityDir`); new pieces queue off the opposite edge.
Drag to turn (a press that moves more than a few pixels is a turn, not a tap), Recenter, or Auto Rotate. Double-clicking or double-tapping in the view but off the board (two taps within 0.4 s and 40 px that both miss it - `View.HitsBoard`, `Main.OnTap`) recenters, the same as the Recenter button.

**Removal rule (2D board only):** removed pieces pop out of the board's own front face (+Z) whichever way the board is turned, then fall under gravity. This is specific to the 2D board. **The 3D cube** launches each piece out of the face it was on (between the faces, for an edge or corner piece), as BoardTestRK's 3D shapes do (`CubeView.LaunchBasis`).

## 2D and 3D

`Main` holds both boards (`Board2D`, `Board3D`) and plays one (`View`, untyped on purpose - both have the same outside); the other is out of the scene. Settings > Board > Board Type switches (it starts a new game on the board switched to, with that board's own saved size: Width/Height/Blockers for 2D, Width/Height/Depth for the cube, saved apart). Settings that both boards share are applied with `Main.Both`; the `BlockStyles` is one object shared by both. The cube's gravity is the world's (the cube turns under it), not BoardTestRK's fixed flow per face. New pieces are drawn falling in from `ArrivalHeight` cells above the cell they are created in (`CubeView.PathPoints`); a cell turned away from above (`ArrivalMinUpward`) has nothing to fall from, so its piece grows into place.

## iOS

`iOS/` holds the Godot iOS export as an Xcode project (product name XBoardTest, team 4BB235M4GL); see `iOS/README.md`. Run it
on a real iPhone, not the Simulator (the 4.7.2 template's simulator library has no arm64 code, which shows up as a missing
`_main`). The folder has a `.gdignore`; its big generated parts are git-ignored. On iOS the scene is drawn at the screen's own
size with 4x MSAA (`UpdateSuperSampling` in Main.gd), and the project plays in landscape and portrait (`window/handheld/orientation=6`, sensor; `Main.UpdateOrientation` switches the stretch base size between 1920x1200 and 1200x1920 and the camera between keep-height and keep-width by window shape). A phone/tablet starts with a 60 per second frame-rate limit (Settings > Performance; `Engine.max_fps`; 0 = no limit elsewhere), since an iPad Pro would otherwise run at 120 and run warm.

**Removal effects** (Settings > Effects; `BoardView.RemovalEffects`, same on the cube): Fly Away, Mini Me, Explode, and three that play in place (`Scripts/View/InPlaceRemoval.gd`, 0.5 s, not scaled by Removal Speed): Disappear (gone at once), Spin and Shrink (spins on a random axis, 1.5 turns, while shrinking to nothing), Spin then Vanish (spins at full size, then gone). A refill waits for them (`InPlaceActive`). **Puff of Smoke** (`Scripts/View/SmokeBurst.gd`, the last effect, `RemovalEffects.Smoke`): the piece is gone at once and soft grey puffs (tinted 30% by the piece's color, 6 per piece for a group of up to 6, one fewer for every 6 pieces beyond that down to 3 - `SmokeBurst.PuffsFor` - because a big group's puffs overlap and only add blending) bloom out in front of its face - toward the viewer on the 2D board, out of the face it was on on the cube - rising exactly against the way pieces fall on that board (`BoardView.SmokeUp`: the board's own gravity - the grid direction the next drop will use - taken into the world, so it follows a tilted board; `CubeView.SmokeUp`: against world down however the cube is turned), swelling and thinning to nothing over `SmokeBurst.DissipationTime` (1.9 s at Removal Speed 1; a typical Explode shard takes 1.7 s, `RestAge + FadeDuration`, measured - the smoke is kept a little longer on purpose; it shortens as Removal Speed rises, like the shards); nearly opaque soon after the start (`Opacity` 0.95). One `MultiMesh` of billboard quads per removal, moved by script from one progress value so they are all exactly gone at the end; a child of `FlyerSystem`, so a new game clears it. The refill does **not** wait for the smoke (nothing is held in a cell). With Smoke chosen, a board that has to be dealt again (no shuffle can make a group; `Recover`) sends its old pieces off in smoke instead of freeing them at once; an ordinary shuffle only moves pieces, so it has no removal effect. A board switched out of the scene while its lightning plays (Settings > Board Type) frees the pieces it was about to send away, whatever the effect (`SendFlying`'s first lines). Test: `Tests/TestSmokeBurst.gd`.

**Attract Mode picks** the largest playable group; ties go to the group closest on average to the bottom (as the user sees it), remaining ties at random (`BoardView.AttractStep`). **On the 3D cube** (`CubeView.AttractStep`) it picks one of the six faces at random (among those with a playable group touching them), then the largest group on that face, so every side gets played; and while Attract Mode is on the cube also turns slowly about all three axes (a Settings > Game switch, "Random rotation in Attract Mode (3D)", on by default and shown only for the cube, turns it off - `CubeView.AttractSpin`; `CubeView.SpinStep`: yaw, tilt and roll each follow their own random sweeps, 0.5-1.5 radians over 6-14 s, eased).

## Naming and style

Same owner conventions as the other projects: PascalCase for everything that is not a Godot API name (functions,
variables, constants, classes, signals, files). Godot's own callbacks keep their names (`_ready`, `_process`,
`_unhandled_input`, `_physics_process`). Keep files reasonably short and split by responsibility.

## Running

Godot is `/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot` (4.7; the project targets 4.6).

```
G=/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot
$G --headless --path . --import                                   # once, after adding scripts (rebuilds the class cache)
$G --headless --path . --script res://Tests/TestBoardModel.gd      # rule tests; prints ALL PASS
$G --headless --path . --script res://Tests/TestGameSession.gd     # the play modes
$G --headless --path . --script res://Tests/TestCubeModel.gd       # the cube's rules
$G --headless --path . --script res://Tests/TestCubeView.gd        # the cube's taps and face turns
$G --headless --path . --script res://Tests/TestSmokeBurst.gd      # the Puff of Smoke removal effect
$G --path . -- --capture=/some/dir                                 # plays itself in Attract Mode, saves screenshots, quits
$G --path . -- --cube --dims=15x3x15 --capture=/some/dir          # the same on the 3D cube (--switch changes board type at frame 200)
```

A script error in a headless test run can still end in `ALL PASS`; read the whole output, not just the last line.
`node_3d.tscn` is the empty scene the Godot editor created with the project; it is not used.

**Zen goal reached**: Zen has no turn/time readout by design (the "Turns left"/"Time" box only shows in Turn/Timed). Once the goal is met in Zen, play can go on and a **Next Level** button (`ControlBar.NextButton`, `Main.OnNextLevelPressed` -> `WinSequence`, hidden while `Advancing`) appears at the bottom center.
Explode refill waits at most `FlyerSystem.ExplodeVacateTimeout` (0.35 s) for shards to clear the cells (was the 2 s timeout whenever a shard lingered); `VacateLimit()` in both views.

**Malicious color switches** (indices 4-6 of `Main.MaliciousKeys`; implemented in `BlockStyles`, tested by `Tests/TestColorModes.gd`): 4 swapped colors (`Scrambled`: `Reshuffle(groups in play)` makes a derangement `Order`, re-rolled at every new game), 5 drained (`Faded`, keeps `FadedKeep` 15% of the hue), 6 all white (`AllWhite`, beats the others). `ShownColor` is what a group is drawn in; `RefreshColors` writes it into the shared `Materials`/Glow materials, so pieces on the board change at once, and switching off restores the real colors. The game logic and the goal badges always use the real Kind/`Palette`; everything that tints from a piece's color (smoke, lightning, hint, veil) uses `Styles.Shown`. Joined shapes still join same-group neighbors, which gives groups away in white mode. `--malicious=4,5` in `CaptureRunner` turns them on (saved like a click).

**Metallic** (Settings > Pieces, under Specular; saved as `Metallic`): `BlockStyles.SetMetallic` -> `ApplyFinish` sets the shared color materials to `metallic` 1.0, roughness `MetalRoughness` (Specular's 0.14 wins if both are on); `SceneSetup.SetMetallic` keeps the reflection sky on (metal is dark without something to reflect). Flyers fade `metallic` out with the shine. Not applied to the Glow shapes. `--metallic` in `CaptureRunner`.

**Splitting big scripts** (GDScript has no partial classes, so a big class is a chain of scripts, each `extends` the one before; only the last has the public `class_name`, so everything outside uses `BoardView`, `CubeView`, `ControlBar` and the `Main` scene script as before). A script may only call functions of the scripts before it in the chain, so the chain runs from building blocks to orchestration; all variables, signals and constants stay in the first script. Chains: `BoardViewTurning` (state, turning, gravity) <- `BoardViewBuild` <- `BoardViewRemoval` (taps, removal effects, Attract Mode) <- `BoardView` (shuffle, fall/refill, `_process`); `CubeView*` the same; `ControlBarRows` (row builders, Open/CloseSettings) <- `ControlBarHud` (readouts, buttons, insets) <- `ControlBarSettings` (the settings window) <- `ControlBar` (`Setup`); `MainScene` (camera, sky, Settings switches) <- `MainMalicious` <- `MainGame` (new game, win sequence, levels) <- `Main` (`_ready`, input, `_process`). Put a new function in the earliest script whose callers can all see it. After adding a script with a `class_name`, run `godot --headless --path . --import` once so the global class list refreshes.

**Rotation speeds**: the Malicious light turns once in 15 s (`Main.LightPeriod`); the 2D board's Auto Rotate turns once in 23.7 s (`BoardView.AutoYawPeriod`), so the two stay out of step. The cube keeps its own 0.3 rad/s.

**Level files** (`Levels/`, read at run time by `Levels.gd`; format in `Levels/README.md`): `PrimaryLevelIndex.json` lists level files; each file has a `Levels` list; a level has Id, Name, Difficulty, Colors, Goals (`Group`/`Remove`), optional Turns/Time (else `TurnsFor`/1 s per piece), optional Shape/RemovalEffect by name, a `Map` of text rows (`.` gap, `#` random piece, `B` Blocker) and an optional `Legend` for more symbols (Gap/Blocker/Piece with `Group` and `Count`). The index is read from `user://Levels/` if it has one (Settings > Game > Copy Levels fills it from the built-in files; Reload Levels re-reads), else `res://Levels/`. Bad files/levels are skipped, printed and kept in `Levels.Problems` (the count and the first problem show in Settings). Difficulty-test results are saved by level `Id` (`user://LevelDifficulty.cfg`, with the turns they were measured for). `LevelDefinition` gained Id/Name/Source/Shape/RemovalEffect and `PresetPieces` {Cell, Kind, Count} is now applied (`BoardModel` last constructor argument; `Deal` re-applies them). `Main.ApplyLevelLook` applies a level's Shape/RemovalEffect unless Settings > Game "Use my Block Shape / Removal Effect, not the level's" is on (nothing is saved; the pickers keep the player's choice). Tests: `Tests/TestLevelFiles.gd` (parsing, rejects, countdown), `TestGameSession` (the shipped files).

**Piece countdown** (`BoardModel.PieceData.Count`, 0 = none; 2D board only, levels only): `Remove` calls `CountDown`: each piece with a countdown touching the removed group along a side goes down by one (once per group removal); at 0 it is taken off the board too (`LastExpired`; the others are `LastTicked`). Expired pieces do not count down their own neighbours. `BoardView.TapCell` sends expired pieces off with the group's removal effect (after the lightning, which is only for the tapped group), updates the numbers (`AddCountLabel`/`UpdateCountLabel`: a billboard `Label3D` child named "CountLabel") and emits `PiecesExpired(Kind, Size)` -> `GameSession.RecordExpired` (counts toward the goal, not a turn, does not start the clock). The difficulty bot counts them too. Blockers are not pieces and never count.

**Separate areas, mid-board gaps and flow** (`Scripts/Model/FlowField.gd`, `Levels.ParseFlow`): a map may have gaps anywhere and several areas of play that do not touch (`BoardModel.Regions`/`RegionCount`/`LargestRegion`; the level only needs one area of 3+ cells; random Blockers - the Malicious relocation too - never raise the number of areas, `PlaceBlockers`). With neither `Flow` nor `Links` in the level the ordinary column drop runs (mid-column gaps seal, `FillDeadCells` fills cells nothing can reach). A flow is **all or nothing** and **overrides gravity**: `Levels.ParseFlow` keeps it only if every open cell has a direction (`v ^ < >`) or a link (`FlowField.CoversEveryCell`); otherwise the whole Flow/Links are ignored with a note in `Levels.Problems` and the ordinary drop runs. When kept, `BoardModel.Flow` replaces `Drop`: the directions are absolute (they do not turn with `GravityDir`, which plays no part), a piece steps to the cell ahead (its link, else the neighbour) if open and empty (round a Blocker diagonally, `Step`); a cell with no inflow is a source and gets a new piece (path from the cell behind it) whenever empty; cells are processed downstream-first (`DownstreamFirst`), a piece never re-enters a cell it visited in the same drop, and `FillDeadCells` is the last resort. Paths are in real cells; a link step is one long step the view tweens straight across. Loops in the flow are rejected at load (`FlowField.Loop`). The flow is for the 2D board only. Samples: `Levels/Flows.json` (Updraft, Islands, Wormholes) and `Levels/GravityOffsets.json` (Crosswinds: rows flowing alternately right and left; Funnel: both sides flow into a middle column that falls; Elevator: a left half flowing up and a link carrying pieces over the top into a right half flowing down). Tests: `TestLevelFiles` (regions, flow up, links, every shipped level x four gravities stays full).
